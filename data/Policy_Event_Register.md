# Policy-event register (supplementary material)

Coded panel of resource-nationalism policy events used to estimate the country-risk activation hazards (Section 2.6). Each row is a specific, dated legal or policy instrument drawn from the public record; the lever is the NPV channel it acts through (E export restriction, N nationalization, L local-content mandate, T tax or royalty). Regime is the documented political-economy classification of the country in that year. The resource-nationalism measures are cross-referenced against the OECD inventory of export restrictions on industrial raw materials (OECD, 2024). Coverage: 41 events across 17 producer countries, 2007 to 2026; the regime and indicator panels add one event-free control country (Canada) for eighteen in all.

[[PEREG]]

Primary-source citations have been added for 40 of the 41 events (final column): statutes, decrees, and statutory instruments where a codified instrument exists, and the authoritative government announcement, cabinet directive, or court/enforcement record where the measure was executive or de facto rather than legislative (for example Chile's National Lithium Strategy, Ghana's Green Minerals Policy, Namibia's June 2023 cabinet directive, Zimbabwe's 2025–2026 ministry directives, Zambia's court-ordered KCM liquidation, and Mali's 2024 seizures). One event remains flagged for the authors: the Zimbabwe 2025 lithium-concentrate export tax, whose rate and enacting instrument are inconsistent across sources and should be confirmed against the Finance Act or national budget statement before submission.

Two coding items to review before submission. First, the Democratic Republic of the Congo cobalt-export suspension is dated 2024 in the panel, but its enacting instrument (ARECOMS Décision Nº 001) is dated 22 February 2025; the year should be reconciled. Second, several rows rest on a single instrument, so their export-restriction and local-content (or tax) facets are two channels of one measure rather than two independent events: Argentina's 2018 export-duty decree (the export-restriction and tax/royalty rows), Ghana's Green Minerals Policy, Namibia's June 2023 cabinet directive, and Zimbabwe's Statutory Instrument 213 of 2022. A robustness check on whether these should be collapsed to one event each is warranted, since the activation hazards are estimated from the event counts.
